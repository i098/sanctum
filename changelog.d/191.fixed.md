Context search answers questions. A query that contains a question or helper word (such as `who`, `the`, `does`, `we`, `us`, `can`, `and`, `not` or a possessive `'s`) is a question. It ignores those words, time words such as `now` and clock times. Its names and other words are its subject, matched by stem; its verb (`who owns`, `was chosen`, `is attending`) and the kind of answer it asks for (`which vendor`, the head of `what is the launch date`, `the owner of onboarding`, the object in `Does onboarding have an owner?`) are optional. A question with a subject lists the items that contain the whole subject, newest first, so `Who owns onboarding now?` lists `Marcus takes over onboarding.` before `Priya owns onboarding.`. A subject of names only (`What is the Falcon mascot?`) lists the named items with the most optional words first. A question without a subject lists the items with one of its verbs, or, when no item has one, the items with one of its answer-kind words, most optional words first; equal matches list newest first, and `next_cursor` keeps that order and choice. A keyword query that contains one of the question or helper words is now read as a question and can match more or other items; other keyword queries and access rules do not change
